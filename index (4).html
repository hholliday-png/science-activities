Math toolkit

ΔH from bond energies, in five moves

  1. Write out each molecule and count every bond. The big number in front of a formula is how many molecules there are.
  2. For each bond type, multiply count × bond energy.
  3. Add the reactant bonds. That total is energy in (breaking bonds takes energy).
  4. Add the product bonds. That total is energy out (forming bonds gives energy off).
  5. ΔH = energy in − energy out. Negative means exothermic; positive means endothermic.

Example: 3H₂ has 3 H–H bonds. 3 × 436 = 1,308 kJ.

Heat: q = mcΔT

qheat energy, in joules (J)
mmass, in grams (g)
cspecific heat, in J/g·°C. Water 4.18, aluminum 0.897, iron 0.449
ΔTfinal temperature − starting temperature

100 g of water warms from 22 °C to 30 °C. ΔT = 8. q = 100 × 4.18 × 8 = 3,344 J.

Smaller minus bigger is always negative: 300 − 500 = −200.
